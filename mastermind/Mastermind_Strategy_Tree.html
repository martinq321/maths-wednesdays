Maths Wednesdays / Strategy explorer

Follow one clue at a time.

Choose a board, open the starting row, then choose the feedback you received. Each branch works out what to try next.

black = exact white = misplaced

Click a clue row to calculate the next guess and unfold its responses. Click it again to close it. Opening a different branch closes the previous path. The page grows naturally — there is no separate scrolling panel.

What does this tree show?

Every branch is computed from all the clues on that path. The number at the right is the number of possible secret codes. A green “only code left” row gives the code to submit. A row with all black pegs means the preceding guess solved the game.

Branches are calculated only when opened. You can explore every possible feedback path without loading a huge full tree.

The classic six-colour, four-hole board uses Knuth’s minimax strategy and is guaranteed to finish in five guesses. For other boards, this app uses verified optimal policies where available, or minimax / sampled suggestions. The strategy note above makes the distinction. This is not Koyama and Lai’s average-optimised strategy.

Optimal worst-case strategies have been checked exhaustively for 32 of the 36 settings. The four exceptions are 4 colours / 6 holes, 5 / 5, 5 / 6 and 6 / 6: their trees are exploratory, not certified optimal. Following another sequence of guesses in the game requires the physical-game helper instead of this fixed-path tree.

* The six-colour/five-hole optimality label uses the later reported lower bound; the six-guess upper bound was independently checked here. The five-colour/six-hole reported optimum is six; this app’s strategy is not certified to meet it.
